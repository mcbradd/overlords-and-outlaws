# Faction frame art direction

Research date: 15 September 2026. Scope: a design brief for the next **generated frame assets**, informed by the six user-supplied card images and primary publisher, museum and palace sources. This is research and an art proposal, not a record of generated assets, visual approval or player testing.

## What the references actually ask for

The user's request governs: original, illustrated family treatments; expansive portrait art; readable live data added by the game. The previous thin pastel edges and flat strips do not satisfy it. “Full art” here means the image and ornamental treatment form a continuous composition, rather than a portrait confined inside a generic interface box. The supplied examples include both open artwork and substantial illustrated frames; their shared strength is **thematic authorship**, not the absence of ornament.

Direct visual observations from the user's six attachments:

| Reference | Observable design choice | Transfer to this game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enlightened Tutor | Rounded, engraved cloud motifs echo the illustrated world; information fields share their shape and material. | A family treatment needs a recognizable drawing vocabulary that also shapes its data field. |
| Mabel, Heir to Cragflame | Fine irregular botanical lines grow around the perimeter; artwork continues beneath the lower text; the face has clear space. | Keep the portrait dominant. Use selective, organic edges and intentionally quiet text regions. |
| Feral Deathgorger | Scales, claws and curling dragon bodies make the frame itself an illustration. | Family identity should remain recognizable when color is removed. |
| Betor, Kin to All | Related draconic geometry pairs with compatible artwork and warm luminous material. | Design ornament and portrait composition together; changing color alone is insufficient. |
| Etali, Primal Storm | A largely open scene carries the card. Angular red trims repeat the image's forceful shapes. | A strong family silhouette can need only a few decisive marks. |
| Jeweled Lotus | Portions of the subject cross the name/rules boundaries; thin fields preserve the dark visual depth beneath. | Permit deliberate art/frame intersections away from text and faces; avoid an opaque lower slab by default. |

These observations concern composition. They are not instructions to reproduce Magic's lettering, icons, artwork or frame drawings. Their upper-right mana and lower-right power/toughness positions do not override the user's bottom-left stats and clear-right-edge requirements.

## What the publisher establishes

Wizards describes **Bloomburrow Showcase Woodland** as a fairy-tale-inspired frame paired with new artwork. The lesson is that an illustrated frame is part of a specific visual world, not a universal outline with a different tint. [Wizards: Collecting Bloomburrow](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/collecting-bloomburrow)

For **Tarkir: Dragonstorm**, Wizards explicitly pairs its dragon-themed showcase frame with matching artwork. Its borderless clan cards also have backgrounds themed to individual clans. These support family-specific motifs and art direction while retaining common game geometry. [Wizards: Collecting Tarkir](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/collecting-tarkir-dragonstorm)

Wizards describes **Commander Masters Frame Break** as art extending through the frame and text box. That directly explains the user's Jeweled Lotus reference: hierarchy survives even when the illustration is allowed to cross the usual partitions. [Wizards: Commander Masters](https://magic.wizards.com/en/products/commander-masters)

**Design inference:** Reserve common locations for data, but generate six independently drawn decorative systems. Let art continue behind those systems. Match each nameplate's scale to the actual portrait crop; a fixed plate crossing a face is a failed composition even if a layout test passes.

## Six families: evidence and proposed treatments

The motifs below are deliberately specific. They are thematic interpretations spanning each game's family roster, not claims that every member used every later emblem.

### Alba — interlaced carving

**Evidence.** Historic Environment Scotland identifies interlace carved into the cross at the Dyce symbol stones. National Museums Scotland also documents an interlace-decorated gaming counter from Rum. Its Lewis chessmen account cautions that the pieces were probably made in Norway and belong to a mixed Gaelic/Scandinavian setting; they should not be labeled uniquely Scottish royal craft. [HES: Dyce Symbol Stones](https://www.historicenvironment.scot/visit/all/dyce-symbol-stones/), [NMS: Lewis chess pieces](https://www.nms.ac.uk/discover-catalogue/the-story-of-the-lewis-chess-pieces)

**Proposed art.** Compact braided/interlaced bands with chiselled, slightly irregular cuts; dark aged bronze or blue-black stone with worn pale-metal ridges. A few strands turn around the lower-left stat field and meet a bottom-center crest socket. Open upper corners and broad central portrait space. Avoid invented tartan covering the image or generic Viking axes.

### Plantagenet — Gothic gilt and broom

**Evidence.** Westminster Abbey records gilt-bronze effigies, stamped broom sprigs, a white hart, and patterned royal imagery on Richard II's tomb. The broom is an unusually specific alternative to undifferentiated gold scrollwork. [Westminster Abbey: Richard II and Anne of Bohemia](https://www.westminster-abbey.org/abbey-commemorations/royals/richard-ii-and-anne-of-bohemia)

**Proposed art.** Slender pointed Gothic cusps, chased old gold, and sparse broom stems branching along the lower corners. Oxblood enamel appears in recesses, not as a whole-card wash. Stronger straight/angular rhythm than Alba. Keep lions/the existing crest as identity; do not apply Richard II's personal white hart to every ruler as a purported historical badge.

### Tudor — heraldic rosework

**Evidence.** Historic Royal Palaces documents Tudor heraldic beasts, green-and-white livery, red dragon ancestry and a gold lion carrying a red-and-white Tudor rose. These were deliberate public assertions of lineage. [HRP: Hampton Court Beasts](https://www.hrp.org.uk/hampton-court-palace/hampton-court-beasts/)

**Proposed art.** Dark botanical stems and small red-and-ivory enamel rose forms, with restrained brass edges. Petals shape the lower-left field, and stems join the central crest setting. This is organic, recognizably floral construction rather than Plantagenet architecture. Use deep green only as local enamel/shadow; no broad green or pastel overlay across portraits.

### Valois — Renaissance cut strapwork

**Evidence.** Fontainebleau's own education service describes Francis I's gallery as a coherent combination of carved walnut, fresco and sculpted stucco. It records cut-leather-like ornament, fruit garlands and the salamander emblem in the associated decorative program. [Château de Fontainebleau: Interior decoration](https://www.chateau-fontainebleau-education.fr/pedagogie/iii-les-decors-interieurs/)

**Proposed art.** Folded and pierced strapwork with pale carved-stucco edges over dark ink-blue recesses; slender gilded fleur-de-lis forms. Small salamander/flame details may be worked into the ornament as a Francis I-era artistic reference, without replacing the common faction crest. The defining shapes are curled cut straps and flattened cartouches, distinct from Bourbon's outward rays. Use airy ornament rather than a thick architectural picture frame.

### Habsburg — articulated imperial metalwork

**Evidence.** The Schönbrunn Group's Habsburg history describes the Golden Fleece as a central Habsburg emblem inherited through Mary of Burgundy and Maximilian. The National Gallery of Art catalogs a 1522/23 bronze medal with the Golden Fleece collar surrounding the FA monogram. [World of the Habsburgs: Golden Fleece](https://www.habsburger.net/en/chapter/golden-fleece), [NGA: Monogram FA and Golden Fleece collar](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/45541-monogram-fa-circled-collar-golden-fleece-reverse)

**Proposed art.** A fine articulated chain, dark burnished metal and warm old gold; feather-like engraving can echo the established eagle crest. The chain's weight naturally collects at bottom center, joining the crest mount. Red enamel accents remain small. Avoid six generic gilded leaf frames; this one should feel linked, ordered and metallic.

### Bourbon — radiating court gilding

**Evidence.** Versailles links Louis XIV's sun emblem to Apollo and notes the use of laurel, lyre and related royal imagery throughout the palace. Its garden account documents gilded sculpture and an extensive solar program. [Versailles: Louis XIV](https://www.chateauversailles.fr/decouvrir/histoire/grands-personnages/louis-xiv), [Versailles: Garden sculptures](https://en.chateauversailles.fr/discover/estate/gardens/sculptures)

**Proposed art.** Fine rays and paired laurel sweeps in luminous aged gilt, with restrained royal-blue enamel at the lower crest setting. Use an outward, open, sunlit rhythm, distinct from Valois strapwork and Habsburg chains. Any acanthus is subordinate rather than an identical generic border. Louis XIV's solar program supplies a family-era artistic theme; it is not every Bourbon's personal arms.

## Generation and integration brief

These are proposed implementation constraints derived from the request and the existing physical-game contract:

1. Generate **text-free raster frame art**, with transparent portrait space and blank, deliberately quiet data fields. Generate the ornament, materials, edges and crest setting; names, role, costs and changing stats remain data rendered in game.
2. Keep one 63:88 silhouette and compatible data anchors across all six families. Family difference comes from drawn structure, material and motif. Do not use CSS borders/gradients as the decorative frame asset.
3. Preserve the broad portrait opening and the complete face/crown silhouette. Prefer a compact lower name field when existing source portraits occupy the top edge. Do not paste a top namebar over a face simply because Magic uses one.
4. Both combat stats stay at bottom left. The existing faction symbol integrates at bottom center. Keep the right-edge information clearance. Decorative marks may continue there, but no unique readable content should depend on that edge.
5. Use a restrained field beneath text that belongs to the generated material treatment. Selectively transparent/darkened art is a compositional option; uniform pastel paint, stacked inset borders and repeated glossy gradients are not the direction.
6. Keep full inspection/reference rules and simplified battlefield faces as separate data presentations. Dynamic values, damage counters, orientation and marriage links stay explicit.
7. Review six actual portraits, including high crowns and long names, at normal play size before expanding. Then inspect opening, action, dense courts and compact screens. Automated rectangle checks cannot establish composition or appeal.

## Research limits

These are primary institutional sources and direct visual observations, not comparative preference testing. The Dyce page was available through search excerpts; direct retrieval returned HTTP 403, so only its stated interlace carving is relied on. No quoted historical material or external image is proposed for direct incorporation. Specific ornament sketches, portrait-safe geometry and the final six assets still require generation and visual review.
