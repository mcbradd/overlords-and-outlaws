## Full supplied email

Hi Bradd,

Thank you for getting back to me so soon on this. As I am fired up to get this project rolling again. This is exactly the conversation I was hoping we'd have.

Your questions are terrific, and rather than bury you in another long email, I'd much rather work through them with you at the table. Interestingly, I've also gone back through some of my original Dynasty development material since writing you, and I think I found something that may help us answer the question about how much history can actually carry the mechanics.
I'm especially interested in kicking around your thinking on OUTLAW leverage, the advance-warning Interregna idea, Eudoxia, and the 13-card Dynasty structure.

A few things I can tell you right off the bat that may be helpful are:

Eudoxia was originally part of the Interregna but I broke her away when I realized that I needed to create a mechanism that discourages players from “hoarding” cards. I want players to focus on building their dynasties under extreme pressure rather than to take the time to be spiteful to other players. So Eudoxia became an external clock that belongs to the game, not to any player.

I’m not locked in on how many puzzles she needs to complete either. At least one but testing may prove we need her to complete all three puzzles. Or maybe we need to invent a fourth?  I am open to find whatever number works best.

I modeled the deck after a deck of playing cards but each dynasty will be asymmetrical.  For example Medicis’ were bankers so they have money influence.  Perhaps a card of theirs will have a banker who can purchase another card from an opposing player or buy their way out of certain Interregna events.  They also had two Popes, so we need to find what superpower Popes carry.  But the Medici were not very powerful warriors, so their army was not as strong as say, the Plantagenets’ who were more warlike but didn’t hold a lot of assets. So each dynasty in their 13 assembly (or however many we decide) will have positives and deficiencies amongst them. Rather than giving every Dynasty the same machinery with different numbers, I'd like their actual historical composition to create strengths, deficiencies and opportunities. This is the type of history I was hoping we could replace with the “arithmetic scaffolding” other TCGs burden themselves with.

Now, I have carefully selected 12 different dynasties (of the 200+) that fit the parameters of the “Blue Blood” (Sanguinis Caerulei) edition to begin the game with, we can select whichever four that you would like to test from them.  This should be fun!

I'm going to work on that dynasty sheet as soon as I send this to you, so  I should have it to you in an hour or so.

The cards in your hands are your OUTLAW cards; that is where your hidden leverage lives. Once you expose Royals on the table, you're declaring your Dynasty and converting concealed possibilities into “visible dynastic position”. So players need to be selective about what they expose and what they keep hidden.

The OUTLAW cards are the ones our players use to leverage, barter, seize, marry or pass on. There is no discard pile either. This fits another game tenet:

“Power is not lost, it is transferred.”

You made a very interesting observation concerning the Interregna that I have not yet considered.  Does it take effect immediately or after the round? Should players be allowed to make one strategic move to delay or defend its calamity?  Very interesting!

Also Carlin has already made mention of O&O to some of the bigger trading card companies out there so she is anxious to get us going on this. So now we have our own “Eudoxia” building her puzzle against us.  Oh the irony!

Let's set up a phone call or a Zoom for later today. Send me a couple of times that work for you and I'll make one work on my end.
Looking forward to it.

Best,
Malachy
