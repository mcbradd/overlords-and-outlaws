# Industry Pitch V1.2 — complete distillation

Source: `OO Industry Pitch V1.2.pdf`, 12 pages, credited to Malachy Murray, 2025.

## Pages 1–2 — promise and fantasy

**Overlords & Outlaws** is a dynasty-building card game about acquiring power, exposing it, and surviving the consequences. The player inherits an already-fracturing bloodline. Kings, queens, conquerors, saints, schemers, bankers, brides, and bastards form a network of blood, marriage, money, office, and ambition. Power exists between people; the throne cannot hold itself. “You were not born to spectate” frames active participation.

## Page 3 — core conflict

Overlords are revealed figures supporting a public claim. Outlaws are the unreadable concealed hand: secrets, bargains, marriages, betrayals, and weapons against exposed dynasties. Exposure creates authority; concealment creates possibility.

## Page 4 — tension

Every gain creates something new to lose. Committing hidden potential creates visible authority and an attack surface. The table should feel like a living map of strength, risk, and opportunity.

## Page 5 — Founders

A dynasty can begin through marriage, order, or force. Founder cards anchor the dynasty, do not grant supremacy, and determine where collapse begins. The slide exhibits three ornate portrait cards, emphasizing distinct foundations rather than a single universal leader pattern.

## Page 6 — philosophy

The two wolves inside a ruler represent violence and desire. Feeding either without restraint is destructive, but neglecting them also costs control. Mastering both is necessary to master power. The visual is an elaborate carved stone relief of intertwined wolves, framing the game in symbolic material craft rather than generic fantasy combat.

## Page 7 — history

Interregna are systemic fractures that affect the whole table. Kingdoms fracture, alliances fail, orders collapse. History belongs to no player. A Blood Edict event illustration reinforces the distinction between people cards and events.

## Page 8 — Eudoxia di Rossi

Eudoxia records the cost of the players' history. Her lost work assembles fragment by fragment. She has no army, bargains, or claim to a throne; she watches. This is an independent non-player victory threat. Her portrait shows a red-haired woman in a warm, gold-lit painterly historical setting.

## Page 9 — experience rhythm

Inherit: assess available people and opportunities. Declare: expose a dynastic claim. Maneuver: marry, bargain, betray, or deploy concealed leverage. Endure: survive opposition, disruption, and exposure. This is an intended strategic rhythm; turn structure remains under development.

## Page 10 — three victory pressures

1. Build a sufficiently strong bloodline.
2. Attack rivals' exposed authority while protecting concealed options.
3. Beat Eudoxia's completion clock.

If her record completes before a dynasty is secure, nobody else wins. The source deliberately supplies no final numerical victory threshold.

## Page 11 — product architecture

A trading card game built from real bloodlines. Each card belongs to a historical lineage; Founders anchor, Royals carry art/lineage/consequence, Interregna fracture the table. The collectible TCG is an entry point; the reusable dynastic engine is the constant. An illustrated bound historical archive reinforces collectibility and archival presentation.

## Page 12 — closing ethos

Inherit carefully, conceal wisely, use ruthlessness when needed, and distinguish possessing power from mastering it. Becoming powerful is only the beginning; holding power is the game.

## Visual system across the deck

Black and near-black backgrounds, thin gold dividers, cream serif typography, restrained crimson accents, historical oil-painting imagery, heraldic decoration, ornate gilded card frames, portraits, old books, and physically crafted surfaces. The digital art direction extends this into a candlelit council chamber with legible interactive layers; the source slides themselves are pitch layouts, not game UI specifications.
