# Game System V1.1 — complete distillation

Source: `OO Game System V1.1.pdf`, 12 pages. Companion development deck, not a finished or balanced rulebook.

## Page 1 — status and provenance

Title: **Overlords & Outlaws — The Game System**. Described as playable core / development presentation / V1.1. The mandate is to finish an unfinished game. Credits: 2025 Malachy Murray. Cover shows a richly painted historical court in a dark, gold-trimmed presentation.

## Page 2 — development status

- In play: inheritance draft, declaration, Overlord/Outlaw split, card circulation, core victory pressure.
- In development: action wording, marriage strength, succession, Founder treatment, collapse procedure.
- Conflicting branches: Royals per dynasty, Interregna count, exact completion pacing.
- Purpose: a clean testing instrument with unresolved design visible. Playable ancestry is not a claim of final balance.

## Page 3 — working components

Four systems act at different levels:

1. **Dynasty cards:** real historical bloodlines. One branch specifies 12 dynasties × 14 Royals; proposed breakdown is one Founder plus 13 other Royals.
2. **Interregna:** events introduced after declaration. Branches have approximately 24 cards; count is provisional.
3. **Eudoxia:** 27 fragments, three paintings, nine fragments per painting; independent victory path.
4. **Outsiders:** people outside bloodline architecture, separate future component, explicitly not Interregna.

## Page 4 — inheritance

Deal eight dynasty cards per player. Each chooses three cards to pass face down clockwise, inspects the resulting hand, passes two, inspects again, and passes one. At least three matching Royals establish a dynasty. Declaration makes inheritance visible. After declaration, normalize the live hidden hand to five cards. Only then add Interregna to active play. The source does not specify a no-matching-triple recovery procedure or precise replenishment order.

## Page 5 — strategic split

Overlords are face-up Royals committed to declared bloodlines. They establish authority and progress while becoming attackable. Outlaws remain concealed and support growth, bargains, trade, seizure, and leverage. Nearing completion requires greater exposure. These are card states, not opposing character races or factions.

## Page 6 — core turn

The strongest recovered rhythm is one consequential decision per turn:

1. Draw or replenish according to the circulation rule.
2. Immediately resolve history if an Interregnum is drawn.
3. Make one strategic move: Build, Barter, Seize, or Marry under the working grammar.
4. Apply reactions and consequences and normalize to five concealed cards where required.

Exact circulation details remain unresolved.

## Page 8 — Alba development artwork

A dynasty is a system, not merely a stack of heroes. The exhibit combines Founders, Unifier, Lawgiver, Warlord, Dynastic Bride, and Intriguer roles; these alter growth, bargaining, recovery, and attack.

The illustrated nine-card development sheet is headed Scottish kings / clans dynasty / Alba and depicts **Kenneth MacAlpin, Malcolm III “Canmore”, St Margaret of Scotland, David I, William the Lion, Alexander III, Robert the Bruce, Marjorie Bruce, and Robert II**. Cards have portrait paintings, heraldry, ornate gold frames, rank numerals, and compact role/rule panels. The deck explicitly warns that artwork, wording, and consistency require a production pass. Native-resolution artwork was inspected; see the illustrated-exhibits appendix for embedded rules and contradictions. The grouping spans multiple Scottish historical houses, an important distinction for the digital archive.

## Page 9 — marriage and succession

Only Queens already in declared dynasties initiate marriage. Each Queen creates one marriage. Marriage links bloodlines; Interregna may break the link. Open questions: how linked cards contribute to completion; treatment of already-played cards; inheritance/succession consequences; edge cases after a link breaks. Marriage must create access in exchange for vulnerability, rather than a flat bonus.

## Page 10 — Interregna

Events, never people. Introduced only after initial declarations. They can break marriages, remove or suppress cards, restructure dynasties, and alter shared conditions. Some systemic, some potentially targeted. Count, severity, and mixture require playtesting. Every dynasty is exposed to history. Illustrated event card: **The Blood Edict**, with a distressed parchment rule panel and dark historical art; the exhibit establishes event presentation, not reconciled production wording.

## Page 11 — Eudoxia

The Witness is a parallel game. All 27 fragments belong to three paintings of nine fragments each. Fragments reveal publicly on draw. If all fragments of any one painting appear before a player secures victory, Eudoxia wins and every dynasty loses. The visual exhibit shows three framed paintings. Players build power; Eudoxia records it. The source does not specify ways to suppress her fragments, draw frequency, or exact simultaneous-resolution precedence.

## Page 12 — next development gate

Open tests: number of Royals, Interregna, cards for completion, and bloodlines; marriage strength; Eudoxia speed. Status: **untested**. The deck is a testing brief for a living system. The design statement is “Holding Power Is The Game.”
