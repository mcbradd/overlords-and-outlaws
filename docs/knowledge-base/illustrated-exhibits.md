Native images were extracted and visually inspected. These details do not appear in ordinary PDF text extraction. Development artwork is evidence of design ancestry, not a reconciled final rulebook.

The Founders heading covers multiple roles, and multiple Founder cards appear inside one dynasty despite the later proposed one-Founder architecture. Distinguish these branches. The exhibit's lower margins are cropped in the embedded image; do not invent missing serials or text.

## The Blood Edict — Game System page 10 and Industry Pitch page 7

- Category: **INTERREGNUM — CALAMITY**.
- Title: **THE BLOOD EDICT**.
- Witness: **Bruno of Segni — jurist who watched law become lineage**.
- Archivist's Mark: a red rose and **Eudoxia di Rossi**.
- Effect: every player must discard one Royal from a bloodline sharing a dynasty with another player's Royal. Then each player controlling a unique dynasty not shared by another player may immediately promote one Royal to Rank III.
- Flavor: “Law did not separate blood. It sorted it.” — Eudoxia’s Record.
- Visuals: gilded sculpted frame; crimson title banner; portrait medallion; parchment rules; Scottish lion and French fleurs-de-lis; a bloodied dagger, broken red wax seal, and old map.

The source prose says Interregna are events, never people. The named witness on the card is contextual flavor, not the event's identity. Rank promotion exists in this illustrated branch but no rank system is reconciled in the recovered core procedure. The digital prototype records that distinction rather than assuming ranks are fully specified.

## Three Founder cards — Industry Pitch page 5

All three have rank **V**, a stone architectural frame, a heraldic shield at top left, title banner, Founder designation, flavor panel, and SANGUIS CAERULUS archive footer (the illustrated spelling differs from Sanguinis Caerulei in the prose).

1. **Rudolf I of Habsburg**, subtitle **Founder of Habsburg**. Quartered red/gold shield. Holds a ring in an autumnal landscape. Flavor: “He did not wage wars. He arranged marriages that outlived empires.” — Eudoxia di Rossi. Footer HABSBURG-01, placeholder serial #0000XX.
2. **Hugh Capet**, subtitle **The Restorer of France**. Blue shield with gold fleurs-de-lis. Crowned and armored, holding a sword. Flavor: “He did not conquer France. He convinced it to stop changing hands.” House Capetian. Footer CAPETIAN-01, #000301.
3. **Charles Martel**, subtitle **The Hammer Before the Crown**. Red shield with three gold crowns. Armored warrior carrying a heavy hammer. Flavor: “He did not ask the world for a crown. He forced it to kneel.” House Carolingian. Footer CAROLINGIAN-01, #000201.

These are game flavor statements, not verified historical quotations. In particular, the depiction of Martel with later heraldic conventions is artistic design, not evidence of period heraldry.
