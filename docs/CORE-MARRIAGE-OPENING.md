# Empty Courts, marriage and initial parity

Current designer decisions, 18 September 2026. These supersede earlier matching-trio declarations and marriage-heir rules.

## Intent

An opening hand with four high cards must not let the first player immediately name an heir and effectively decide the game. Founding, marriage and Claim each spend a separate turn, exposing the Court to opponents between steps. Mechanical Dynasty differences are deferred until later thematic tuning.

## Procedure

1. Deal eight and retain the simultaneous clockwise 3–2–1 draft.
2. Finish drafting with eight private cards, no Dynasty, no Ruler and no Nobles in Court. No matching-trio declaration or repair is needed.
3. Recruit any Noble into an empty Court. That Noble establishes its Dynasty and becomes Ruler.
4. On a later turn, Marry an opposite-gender Noble from hand to the Ruler. Rank and Dynasty do not restrict eligibility. A Ruler has one spouse at a time. This does not create an Heir or Claim.
5. With that spouse present, a later Claim plays an own-Dynasty Noble from hand as Heir. Keep the original Ruler and Heir through the whole next round to win. Marriage is an entry requirement, not a third hold dependency.
6. If the Ruler leaves Court, their surviving spouse immediately becomes Ruler. The Court retains its established Dynasty. Their marriage ends; the successor must remarry before another Claim. Removing the original Ruler still breaks any existing Claim.

All four thirteen-card Dynasties have female Nobles at 6, 7, 8, 10, J, Q and male Nobles at A, 2, 3, 4, 5, 9, K. Historical identities and artwork vary; gameplay eligibility and rank comparisons do not. See [roster and artwork record](art/CORE-ROSTER-PARITY.md).

The storage ruleset is versioned because old rosters and old pending-Challenge zones are incompatible. Existing bytes are not silently reinterpreted. Routine play and tutorial no longer display storage notifications.

## Presentation

Trade and Challenge display their two cards side by side, with response buttons below. Challenges leave the player's hand available. The Challenger occupies the pending interaction until resolution, then enters the correct Played pile. Arrow endpoints follow the visible source card, including the centered Challenger. Ending an interaction clears and hides the arrow canvas.

The surrounding wooden council surface shares the board camera. Tutorial guidance stays on an independent layer in the normal layout. Selected cards remain green; interaction prompts remain yellow. Inspection uses the dim viewport directly, with a card and unobtrusive controls. Physical card corners use a 2.5:63 radius ratio.
