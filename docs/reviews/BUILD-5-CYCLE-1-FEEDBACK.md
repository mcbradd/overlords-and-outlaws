## Completed screen-only cohort

The fresh learner had only the published screen and UI, without source, rules documents, storage or engine access. It completed the tutorial and correctly explained the succession objective, full following reign round, consecutive-pass boundary, card return/draw, higher same-Dynasty defense and concealed threat. It reported a long, repetitive lesson of roughly 75 clicks and ten minutes. Trade, Queen role and the 12-round cap were not learned there.

All ten ordinary games reached a terminal result: eight wins and two losses. The cohort includes seven duels, two three-player games and one four-player game, across all four current Dynasties. All used seat 1 because the observer found no seat-choice control; this is not evidence of balanced starting seats.

Counts and durations are the observer's estimates, not instrumented telemetry. Terminal screenshots were opened in the observer's browser-tool transcript; the API supplied no saved screenshot paths. The player reported a possible visual rank mismatch for Matilda of Scotland and Duncan I in game 1. Later observations showed correct ranks, and game 6 demonstrated that an immediate accessibility snapshot and screenshot could capture different transition moments; a settled recapture agreed. The initial rank report remains unconfirmed, with no demonstrated engine cause.

These games establish concrete opportunities to use public information and recover from setbacks. They also show repeated low-heir/high-defense play, native-threat removal through exact Trade, lengthy multiplayer interleaving and suit-dependent inability to contest some imminent wins. They do not establish balance, absence of dominant strategies or human enjoyment. This is one fresh agent completing ten games, not ten independent people or a family study. Its separate sixth-grade assessment must accompany the next design decision.

The completed assessment says the ordinary screen does not let a new sixth-grade reader confidently predict each move with minimal text. It identifies rule complexity as well as presentation: Recall compares the lead and answer rather than the target; Trade adds name lookup, return timing and a special immediate deployment; succession switches which three/two people are required across two stages; marriage separates Queen role from Q rank; Pass does not end participation and resets after another action. Legal-action filtering allowed clicking before the player could predict the full result. That is evidence against treating enabled buttons as comprehension. Exact Trade also became a reliable way to remove known threats when the AI accepted; opponent policy and underlying bargain incentives need separate review.

The next panel must preserve the value of conditional choices without preserving their current machinery. The whole-game gauntlet records a reducer-tested local counterexample to universal lowest-heir dominance and rejection of two proposed replacement attack rules. These are comparative design evidence, not reasons to accept the current succession clock or defer the user's clarity requirement.
