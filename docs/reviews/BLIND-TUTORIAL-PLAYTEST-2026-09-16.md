# Blind tutorial playtest — September 16, 2026

## Method and result

One adult AI surrogate attempted to reason in plain, sixth-grade-level language as a first-time player. This is not testing with a child and does not establish child comprehension. The agent used only the visible browser UI at https://mcbradd.github.io/overlords-and-outlaws-prod/. No repository files, source, hidden game state, network, console, storage, DOM attributes, or other agents' findings were consulted. Visible screenshots were the primary evidence; UI snapshots supported interactions and visible wording. Offscreen glossary content was scrolled into view before use. No hints, suggestions, recommended plans, or advice controls were used.

Started with **Learn at the table**, completed all **59 tutorial steps**, and reached a visible **round 4 win**. End screen: **“THE CROWN IS SETTLED”**, **“You prevails”**, and **“You wins under Recognition of the Kindreds. Robert the Bruce stayed Ruler for 1 full round(s).”** Screenshots of opening, actions, inspections, succession, and victory are in the browser tool transcript. No new match was started. The end-screen “Predict the next consequences” button was not used because it could be an advice or quiz control.

Completion establishes that the prescribed tutorial sequence was navigable. It does **not** establish independent strategic competence: most later actions were single buttons that performed the guide's named action and chose its targets automatically.

## Chronological observations

### Opening and inheritance — steps 1–12

- Title: “Recruit a dynasty. Keep your intentions concealed. Let your government outlive its ruler.” I did not yet understand the win condition. “Learn at the table” was the obvious beginner choice.
- A “PRIVATE TABLE HANDOFF” screen addressed “You” and told me to pass the device, despite my starting alone. “Open my view” was understandable and worked.
- Step 1: “Choose these 3 Nobles to pass left.” It named Eleanor of Aquitaine, Richard I, and Elizabeth I. Footer said “clockwise”; I assumed these meant the same direction but was not sure. I expected to give away three cards and receive three.
- I selected the three visible hand cards. “Lock selection” produced “You locks their passing packet.” Cards remained until rivals also locked. Each rival needed “Resolve opponent action” and then “Continue.”
- Step 3 produced “All 3-card packets pass clockwise.” I received Alexander II, Alexander III, and Edward II. This clarified the simultaneous exchange.
- Step 4: “You can pass cards you just received. Try to keep 3 Nobles with the same Dynasty name.” This was the first strategy explanation. It ordered Alexander II and Edward II passed. I would otherwise consider passing Tudor Henry VIII instead of Alba Alexander II to retain more Alba cards; the guide did not explain its choice.
- Step 7 ordered Henry VIII passed. That choice fit my emerging idea of keeping Alba cards. Edward III arrived after the final exchange.
- Step 10: “Kenneth, Margaret and David make Alba your Dynasty. Another player may also choose Alba; each keeps their own Court.” I selected those three. I guessed Court meant people on my side. Declarations waited for all players and put three cards into each Court; five remained in my hand.
- Passing was clear by the end: three cards, then two, then one, with simultaneous resolution. Ten steps before declaring felt repetitive. The guide still had not given the win goal or explained seals.
- At the browser's normal large viewport, the board occupied much of the screen with tiny labels and extensive empty space; hand names were readable but Inspect buttons initially fell below the fold. A short ordinary scroll revealed them.

### Rulers, History, and Trade — steps 13–21

- Step 13: “Give Kenneth the Ruler marker.” Body said “Choose who leads your Court,” but “Appoint Ruler” directly chose Kenneth. No target choice was presented. The resulting RULER label was tiny; result text confirmed success. I understood each player could have a Ruler while nobody held the Crown.
- Step 15 said “The first public History draw happens now, after Inheritance.” Result: “The Kindreds at Scone · 1 is revealed.” At the same time two warnings appeared, Contested Recognition and Border Rising, and History fell from 27 to 24. I could not explain why this first draw involved three cards from the explanation alone.
- Step 16: “Read Edward III. You can Trade him, or Lend him to Recall a rival’s Plantagenet Noble. Here, offer him to The Lion.” I independently inspected Edward and expanded “Words and actions on this card” because Recall and Lend were unfamiliar.
- The card and its glossary explained Court, Bloodline, seals, Recall, Block, Trade, Lend, Cover, and Challenge. I scrolled through the long glossary. I learned that seals are action tokens, three reset each round; a loan returns next round; a Recall can take a matching-dynasty rival Court card unless blocked; and any complete uncovered six-piece painting makes everyone lose. This inspection was useful but introduced many terms at once.
- “Offer Trade” automatically offered Edward without selecting the card or rival. Step 17 title “The Lion locks Richard” supplied Richard's identity before the later agreement-to-look steps.
- Steps 17–19 repeatedly returned the old outcome “You offers 1 Noble in hand to The Lion,” even after consent actions. Step 19 said “Now both players can look,” but no offered Richard card or trade inspection area appeared on the visible screen.
- Step 20: “Richard belongs to Plantagenet. Marry him to your Queen to bring him into your Court, or keep him in hand for a Recall.” I could not explain why swapping Plantagenet Edward for Plantagenet Richard was beneficial. The only action offered was “Accept exchange”; no decline choice was visible.
- Step 21 actually swapped Richard into my hand and reduced seals from three to two. The cost and need for both accepts were clear.

### Block, marriage, and first round end — steps 22–30

- The Lion lent an Alba card to Recall Margaret. I could explain why I wanted to protect her: spend one seal and temporarily lend an Alba hand card so she stays.
- Step 23 “Block” automatically selected William the Lion. It did not ask which matching card to lend or offer letting the transfer happen. William left my hand, Margaret stayed, and one seal remained.
- Margaret was labeled “RECALLED THIS ROUND” after the successful Block. This sounded as though she had been taken, although she visibly stayed. The guide's result “The Block prevents the transfer” was clearer.
- Step 24 showed the Rose spend a seal and turn Elizabeth of York sideways to contribute 1 of 2 to Border Rising. I understood two different people were needed, but had not inspected the crisis penalty.
- Step 25 “Choose the actual marriage” again offered one button. “Marry” automatically paired Margaret and Richard, moved Richard into my Court, and spent the final seal. I understood that the marriage made this foreign person count in my Bloodline while it lasted.
- Step 26 the Lion's John supplied the second Challenge and Border Rising disappeared: “Border Rising is averted.” Step 27 the Rose recruited Mary I.
- Steps 28–30 repeated “Pass costs nothing. You can act later if someone acts. The round ends when everyone passes in a row.” Passing with zero seals made sense. Three consecutive passes ended the round.
- At round 2, William returned, seals refreshed to three, Robert II appeared in my hand, and three painting pieces appeared. The result only said “You draws one Noble in hand,” omitting most visible changes.

### Active crisis, Cover, and learning the goal — steps 31–43

- Step 31 finally explained that the unresolved Contested Recognition “now blocks Crown claims” and that lending a card of one's own Dynasty helps end it early. Lion and Rose each lent a card; the counter reached 2 of 3 players.
- Step 33: “Put Malcolm in The Past to Cover Alba’s first painting piece. Uncover it at the start of round 4. Malcolm never returns.” Cost and duration were clear. The Cover button automatically discarded Malcolm and covered the named piece. With Alba only at 2/6, I would probably save that permanent sacrifice until danger was nearer; no reason for the timing was explained.
- Steps 34–35 and 37–38 showed rivals passing, then my action resetting the consecutive-pass counter to zero. This was a useful demonstration that passing did not prevent later turns.
- Step 36: “Recruit Robert the Bruce. Alba’s two named heirs must be from different printed branches.” I did not yet know the claim requirements, so independently opened **Your Law**.
- The law “Recognition of the Kindreds” explained: three own-Dynasty Court cards including Ruler; name two other own-Dynasty Court people from different branches; keep the old Ruler and at least one heir until the Ruler changes; “In 1 round, at its start: Retire your Ruler. Crown a remaining named heir”; then keep the new Ruler for one full round to win.
- I interpreted the awkward timing sentence as changing ruler next round and surviving that round. Now recruiting Bruce made sense: Margaret and David were both Dunkeld, while Bruce was Bruce–Stewart. Robert II in hand seemed an alternative for the same branch role.
- The Recruit button chose Bruce automatically. Step 39 Help automatically lent Alexander III and ended Contested Recognition, leaving zero seals. I understood removing a claim restriction, but the crisis label said it ended after round 2 anyway and I could no longer afford a claim that round. I could not explain paying to end it now versus waiting.
- Steps 40–43 were further passes and a round refresh. The result again emphasized a hand draw without explaining all refreshed resources and paintings.

### Claim, prescribed failure, and recovery — steps 44–56

- Step 44 explicitly named David and Bruce as heirs and said to keep Kenneth and at least one heir until round 4. “Claim the Crown” automatically chose those heirs and spent a seal. No heir selectors were shown. Margaret seemed a possible alternative Dunkeld heir from the law I had read.
- Step 45 the Lion tried to Recall Kenneth. At step 46 the guide said “Let this attempt fail” and “Choose not to Block.” Only “Decline Block” was offered.
- **I would have blocked**: I had two seals and four Alba cards and wanted to keep my claim. Declining was a prescribed teaching action, not my voluntary strategic mistake. I followed it to learn recovery.
- Kenneth left and the Crown became vacant. The outcome was “1 player must choose before this effect resolves,” which did not clearly name the failed claim or required next choice.
- Step 47 “Choose an interim Ruler” automatically locked David for free. I understood replacing a missing Ruler was different from a winning succession.
- Step 49 automatically recruited Alexander III to “rebuild your Court.” I could not explain why this was necessary: David, Margaret, and Bruce already appeared to satisfy the law, with Margaret/Bruce as different-branch heirs.
- Step 52 then did choose Margaret/Bruce, reinforcing that question. The claim spent my last seal while opponents had actions remaining. That felt vulnerable after the previous Recall lesson. Rivals passed instead of testing the weakness.
- Step 55 advanced to round 4 and uncovered the old covered piece. Step 56 then said “First uncover the painting piece due now. Then choose Robert as Ruler and put David in The Past.” The uncovering had already happened on the prior screen.
- “Lock choice” automatically chose Bruce, removed David, added a new hand card, and revealed History. Result again said only “You draws one Noble in hand.” The guide correctly explained that retaining Bruce until the round ended would win.

### Victory — steps 57–59

- I could justify passing with three seals and four Alba hand cards: preserve defense and let the winning round end. No crisis was active; Alba was 4/6, other paintings 3/6.
- Both rivals passed with all three seals available. There was no actual final defensive test.
- The last resolution immediately displayed “You prevails” and confirmed Bruce had stayed Ruler for one full round. The tutorial and match were completed. No hint or postgame advice control was used.

## What I could explain unaided at the end

- Keep matching family cards for recruitment, declaring a Dynasty, and defending against Recalls.
- Save a seal and a matching hand card to Block a threat to a required Ruler or heir.
- Public Court cards can help build a claim and face threats; hidden hand cards retain options.
- A foreign card can support a matching Recall or enter my Bloodline by marriage to my Queen.
- Lending ties a card up temporarily; discarding to The Past loses it permanently.
- Challenges and lending can address shared crises, sometimes requiring different people or all players.
- Passing is free, can be followed by later action, and three consecutive passes end this three-player round.
- Alba's claim needs two different heir branches, a lawful ruler change, and one surviving round under the new Ruler.
- Full uncovered paintings threaten all players; Cover delays a piece rather than removing it permanently.

These explanations came partly from my voluntary card-glossary and law inspections, not solely from the tutorial guide.

## What remained uncertain

- Why several prescribed moves were worth their costs: same-Dynasty Edward/Richard trade, early Cover at 2/6, ending a restriction just before its natural end with no remaining claim seal, and recruiting Alexander before naming other heirs.
- Whether any same-role, same-Dynasty Noble differs strategically beyond the visible branch or Queen status.
- How I would select legal targets in a normal match. The guide mostly chose cards, rivals, spouses, heirs, and successor for me.
- Exactly how normal History draws are counted and ordered; the visible results often reported only the last hand draw or one revealed card.
- Details of uninspected crises, other Dynasties' laws, and a competitive opponent's choices. These were not learned merely by completing Alba's script.
- The physical meaning of tiny board indicators and where persistent Loans/The Past could be examined. Result text was much easier to read than board labels.

## Blockers and errors

There was **no hard gameplay blocker** and no accidental game action requiring recovery. One detached-button automation failure occurred during the second packet selection; checking the current screen showed the intended cards selected, and clicking the visible Lock button finished it. This is a tool interaction issue, not evidence of player confusion or a game rule bug.

There were **comprehension blockers**: unfamiliar terms before inspection, a late win-goal explanation, missing/stale outcomes, and prescribed choices without sufficient reasons. Independent inspections resolved several vocabulary and goal questions. The deliberate failed Crown attempt was required by the tutorial and must not be scored as a voluntary mistake.

The strongest supported conclusion is that I could finish the guided match and explain several core rules by the end, but the tutorial's many automatic named actions did not test my ability to choose and carry out a strategy independently.
