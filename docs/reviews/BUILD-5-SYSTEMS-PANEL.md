Status: adversarial design recommendations for parent synthesis, not implementation or observed player evidence. These are simulated systems, accessibility, exploit-analysis and physical-game perspectives; no professional consultation or human test is claimed. Read against the latest user transcript, designer-intent.md, SUIT-AND-RANK-DESIGN.md and HISTORY-ENGINE-IMPLEMENTATION-SPEC.md. The latest transcript supersedes seals and universal action menus. ## Round 1 — attack the inherited design

**Accessibility perspective:** R4 adds rank comparisons to ten already unexplained orders. It improves tactical specificity without repairing the first-time player's dependency problem. Reject introducing loans, recurring events, painting fragments, four succession institutions, readiness and trade procedure before the learner can explain ruler, hand, opponent and win condition.

**Systems perspective:** Merely deleting seals makes Draw, Withdraw and pass/re-entry potential unlimited loops. Each accepted initiation must irreversibly consume an available card or finite deck capacity during its round. Every defensive answer must remove its answering card too. A response does not create a response chain.

**Physical-game perspective:** A 52-card deck, public discard/spent areas, crown and named heir/witness markers suffice for the first core test. No hidden computed score, secret rank threshold, counterfeit genealogy, damage or fungible action currency is needed. Printed suit/rank is immutable. Rank is an authored game allocation, not historical merit or chronology.

**Exploit perspective:** Assigning Claim exclusively to Q/K or Recall exclusively to a small rank band risks games decided by receiving the right verb. A stronger card cannot be best at everything. If recruiting a high card means losing the strongest available defensive answer, rank can matter through opportunity cost without a second recruitment equation.

**Disposition:** ship a clearly labeled minimal succession core only after its complete gates pass. Retain History/Eudoxia and institutional Laws as deferred design, not silently deleted and not an old seal engine presented as compatible full play. Do not add them to the introduction.

## Round 2 — proposals, objections, adoption

Proposal A assigns thirteen unrelated abilities to thirteen ranks. Accessibility objects that this replaces ten menu verbs with thirteen exceptions and random access to essential actions. Systems cannot guarantee recovery without universal fallback rules. Rejected for the first core.

Proposal B assigns rank bands to Draw, Trade, Recall, Withdraw and succession. Exploit analysis identifies scarce Recall and unique crown keys as accidental immunity and hand lottery. Rejected as exclusive permissions. Future optional tactics may use bands only after the same minimal game demonstrates worthwhile decisions.

Proposal C prints the small common action vocabulary on each person and uses suit/rank for admission, target connection and response strength. The ordinary choice is expose this person to develop the dynasty, or spend their concealed connection to challenge another dynasty. Accessibility supports it because the player examines two held cards instead of an always-present verb menu. Systems supports it because exact cards pay for exact actions. Adopt for a first testable core, with the limitations stated below.

## Concrete core candidate for synthesis

1. Two to four seats; each begins with its native Founder as ruler and two private hand cards. Tutorial uses a fixed legal deal; ordinary games use a documented reproducible shuffled setup. The tutorial deals one useful native and one useful foreign card. Do not quietly apply an undisclosed tutorial guarantee to ordinary random play.
2. Card face presents full name, printed dynasty/suit, A–K rank, portrait and its available instructions. Each native hand card permits **Recruit**: move this card upright to your Court. Each hand card permits **Recall** when a rival Court contains its printed suit: commit this hand card face up to Spent and name one rival Court card of that suit. Neither current owner nor marriage alters printed suit.
3. Recall does not compare against the target rank. The target's controller may **Defend** once using a hand card of that same printed suit with strictly higher rank than the Recall lead. Ace also answers J/Q/K, and does not answer 2–10. Commit the answer to Spent. No counter-answer. If unopposed, move the target to the attacker's Spent area for return at next boundary; it is not immediately playable. Resolve removed offices/dependencies immediately. Mark that target as already attempted this round so repeated target attacks cannot exhaust every answer at once.
4. A card in hand may therefore be worth exposing as a native supporter, spending to remove a rival connection, or retaining as a particular ranked answer. A large lead is harder to answer but commits a valuable defense; low leads can draw out larger answers. Ace has a narrow answer to face cards rather than unconditional superiority. These are hypotheses to test, not proven strategy.
5. With a native ruler and one other native supporter already in Court, playing a further native hand card may **Name heir** instead of Recruit. This puts that card into Court, records the existing supporter as Witness, and claims the vacant Crown for the current ruler. Thus the initial leader plus one hand play cannot claim. All three named cards are distinct. Every native rank can qualify; no unique Queen, King, gender, invented lineage or high-card set is required.
6. The ruler, heir and Witness must remain supported and in that seat's Court through the claim. The Crown passes to the named heir at the next round start. The former ruler remains Court support; require the named Witness throughout the successor's full reign round. Removing the heir, current crowned ruler or Witness fails the attempt immediately and returns the Crown to its neutral place. Repairs do not restore a failed claim. Another lawful attempt is possible on a later turn. A successor wins only at that round's end, after all players have had their ordinary opportunity and any pending response is resolved.
7. Use the current reversible **Pass** procedure: pass retains concealed answers and the player may act if play comes around again. N consecutive passes end a round; any committed legal initiation clears that count. Invalid input changes nothing. If a response is pending, no pass or round boundary bypasses it. Pass and Exit are table procedures, not a consumable card invented just to make every UI button a card ability.
8. No seals. Each ordinary action removes its initiating card from available hand to Court or Spent. Spent cards and captured targets are unavailable until the round boundary. At the boundary return committed cards to their current entitled owner's hand, then replenish hands below two from the finite common deck, then resolve scheduled crown transfer and begin ordinary turns. The next iteration may choose discard rather than return, but must state that choice consistently across rule text, engine and UI before coding. Returning cards preserves people and a visible rhythm; permanent discarding needs an explicit recovery design when the deck empties.
9. Empty deck means no replenishment, not a surprise reshuffle. A rulerless seat may Recruit a native card as its new ruler. Persistent no-progress states and prototype duration limits must be explicit: recommended public twelve-round prototype cap ends as **Unsettled Crown**, never a fabricated player win or Eudoxia loss. This cap is a test parameter, not evidence of a satisfying game. Record cap outcomes separately and revisit if frequent.
10. Draw is ordinary round replenishment in this core; Trade, Withdraw, marriage, Help, Challenge, Cover and full History remain deferred. Do not expose disabled unexplained buttons. Reintroducing any as a card ability requires its complete cost, target, outcome, rank/suit consequence, empty-state behavior and tests before implementation. In particular, paid Draw must consume a card and finite deck supply; Withdraw must not return a immediately playable card while leaving its payment reusable.

## Round 3 — attack the adopted core

**Objection: identical native Recruit instructions fail thirteen-rank uniqueness.** Defense: exact rank changes the defensive answer surrendered and the later Recall available if ownership changes. No rank is cosmetic. However, if measured play always recruits the lowest native and saves the highest, the implementation fails the agency gate despite being legal. Do not add arbitrary ready/sideways rules merely to claim each verb contains arithmetic. Future rank-linked tactics must solve an observed limitation.

**Objection: the Crown is a race to three natives.** Accepted risk, not dismissed. Requiring exposed Witness creates an additional vulnerable dependency, and suit-matched recalls can disrupt it. Count uncontested claims and always-recruit policy performance. If a passive three-native race dominates, revise before expanding art or History.

**Objection: the attacker repeatedly Recalls a Founder, preventing meaningful turns.** Target once-per-round limits repeated drains; rulerless recovery remains explicit. Measure practical elimination and back-to-back ruler losses. Ruler immunity is rejected because it removes the user's desired vulnerable authority. If hand access makes recovery implausible, revise draw/setup, not secretly protect tutorial opponents in free play.

**Objection: spending cards removes agency rather than seals.** Accepted tradeoff to measure. The same two cards supporting initiative and defense makes the user's requested resource concrete, but could yield long pass stretches. Record turns with zero actionable cards, choice count, and whether waiting allows meaningful defense.

**Objection: narrow core abandons historical richness.** Defense: label it accurately as the succession core under test. Preserve art, identity, source material and deferred History specification. Do not claim the full History vision is finished. Expansion begins only when the foundational decisions survive observed play.

## Required tests before any selected rule is considered complete

- **Identity:** all 52 explicit suit/rank allocations unique; exactly thirteen ranks per suit; immutable across transfers, save/reload and previews; no historical gender/title inferred from rank.
- **Costs and legality:** illegal Recruit/Recall/heir/answer are atomic no-ops; one initiation removes exactly one available hand card; every answer committed exactly once; spent cards cannot act again this round; targets cannot be attempted twice this round.
- **Answer table:** same-suit 3 answers 2; equal 3 fails; foreign K fails; same-suit A answers J/Q/K and fails against 2–10; same-suit 2 answers A. Use actual distinct physical IDs in every fixture.
- **Card conservation:** every card occupies exactly one zone after legal action, failed response, removal, boundary, restored save and terminal state. Captured targets return to the correct new owner and never become immediately playable.
- **Round finiteness:** generate maximum legal hands and adversarial action choices. Every initiative decreases the number of available cards or finite deck stock. No legal action replenishes available cards within the round in the proposed core. Reversible pass cannot make a round infinite once all finite commitments finish and every seat passes. Pending responses resolve before boundary.
- **Succession:** ruler plus one new recruit cannot claim; ruler plus existing supporter plus new distinct heir can; every native rank qualifies; foreign heir fails; transfer occurs only next start; remainder of claim round never counts as reign; heir/Witness removal fails immediately; reinserting a removed dependency cannot revive claim; all remaining players act before successor victory.
- **Recovery and cap:** empty hand, empty deck, no ruler, no native hand, last native in a rival Court, all passes, depleted court and cap coincidence with legitimate victory. Define terminal precedence: legal successor win after complete round checks, otherwise cap draw. No stale pending choices survive terminal.
- **Information:** opponents see counts, public suits/ranks and commitments only; no concealed hand IDs, next draws or private AI candidate traces in public projections, DOM, console, accessibility tree or record export. A response option absent from another player's view cannot reveal a held rank.
- **Agent policy:** use the same observable projection as humans; known past disclosures may inform decisions. Compare always-recruit, always-hoard, largest-lead, smallest-sufficient-answer and mixed policies. A deterministic league is supporting evidence, not a blind human playtest.
- **Observed ten-game record:** seed, seats, tutorial exposure, duration, rounds, winner/draw, Crown attempts, defensive answer decisions, stalled turns, instruction lookups and at least one counterfactual per game. Flag automatic choices, not merely completed games. Do not assert entertaining outcomes from completion counts.
- **Screen-only tutorial:** an agent with no design/repository information explains objective before first taught action and again after the teach, predicts the next crown boundary, points to its two available cards and what opponents could conceal, then plays unaided. Record exact misunderstandings and actual screen interactions. The design-writing agent is not a blind evaluator.

## Cross-review of integrated production proposal

Read BUILD-5-EXPERIENCE-PANEL.md and BUILD-5-PRODUCTION-PLAN.md after the first systems report. This is an additional actual document review; no game has been run in this review.

### Definitive minimum recommendation

Keep Recruit, Recall, Defend and Name heir as the complete first core. Keep replenishment and reversible Pass as public timing procedures. Explicitly defer Scout, Trade, Withdraw, Marry, Help, Challenge and Cover, naming their affected production tests as deferred rather than silently treating them as covered. All thirteen ranks have a contextual card ability and the common ranked answer. That is a smaller test of the user's central hypothesis than nine concurrent verbs. The normal hand offers card-specific choices; the tutorial exposes only the one taught action. A global ability menu is not reintroduced.

Reject the integrated proposal's readiness rule for this first core. With an Ace Founder, every other native enters sideways; this does not meaningfully distinguish native ranks during ordinary initial development. Unless another action actually tests readiness, orientation would be pure bookkeeping. Do not retain it merely because R4 did. If readiness is retained, the binding spec must name its downstream effect and test a position in which lower versus higher recruitment changes a real available action.

Court rank in this minimum has a precise opportunity cost: a public person cannot be the concealed higher-ranked answer or lead. That is a real system consequence, but NOT evidence that rank distinctions produce sufficient court-building strategy. The existing plan requires observing and revising compulsory lowest-native recruitment. This limitation must remain explicit in the binding design; do not claim a unique tactical ability for each rank when the algorithm is shared.

If the production owner decides this consequence is inadequate before the first test, the smallest additional court-rank rule is: a claim with a native Witness lower than its nominated heir needs one full successor round; every other valid claim needs two. Every native rank still has a legal path, the schedule is public and frozen at declaration, and exposing the small Witness creates a meaningful vulnerability. This is a reviewed alternative, not a simultaneous extra rule. It costs a second clock concept and should not be introduced without exact UI/tutorial/tests. Do not combine it with readiness just to add more rank effects.

The claim contract must say exactly when the former ruler stops being a required dependency. Recommend: all named people remain required until transfer; after transfer only the new ruler and named Witness remain required. Removing the former ruler afterward does not retroactively fail succession. This is easier to explain than forever preserving all three. Claiming round is never a reign round; a complete protected successor round is required.

Lock circulation as **return committed and captured cards at the next round boundary, then draw only enough to bring hands below two up to two, without discarding larger hands**. No player can play a card immediately after it is captured, traded or withdrawn in the same round. Empty deck does not reshuffle. The public twelve-round prototype limit results in an Unsettled Crown draw if no valid win is achieved first. Record its use, and treat frequent cap draws or repeated frozen hands as failures requiring a new written plan. A cap proves termination, not healthy pacing or attainable victory.

### Adjudication of proposed extra rank-band tactics

These are not approved for first-core implementation by this review. If selected by parent synthesis, each needs the following completed definitions/tests before code begins:

- **A–4 Scout:** commit the actor; deal up to two ordered face-down cards; keep original first without disclosure, or reveal and keep original second only when its rank is at most the actor's. Return the unused card to the deck bottom. Empty deck disallows Scout before cost; a one-card deck takes that card. Do not permit taking an arbitrarily chosen qualifying card while concealing whether the original ordering was preserved. The monotone measure H+D falls by one: the actor leaves hand, one card enters hand, deck loses one. Test first/second ordering, equal threshold, one/zero deck, no reveal of unchosen identity, and no actor return this round.
- **5–8 Trade:** a recipient-chosen face-down card is a different game from requesting an exact rank. Do not mix them. Prefer one binding exact suit/rank request with the offered actor revealed; recipient can decline without indicating whether held. Accepted exact cards both go to their new owners' Spent areas until next boundary. Decline counts as Pass and ends this opportunity; once-per-recipient ledger resets next round. As written, rank does not affect the bargain except access to the verb and card opportunity cost. Adding a received-rank threshold would compound luck and narrow agreement. Test no accept-then-withdraw, absent requested card, fake substitution, rejection privacy, owner return, repeated requests and all-pass closure.
- **9–J Withdraw:** commit actor and move one own Court target ranked at most actor to Spent. Resolve its office/marriage/Crown loss immediately. Returning the target directly to playable hand is rejected. Test equal/lower/higher target, removed heir/Witness, failed claim not restored, self-target impossibility (actor is in hand), and no same-round return. High Court cards Q/K cannot be withdrawn through this band; publish that limitation rather than granting a hidden universal fallback.
- **Q/K Marry:** the proposal must choose whether printed Queen role matters, whether actor is native or foreign, and precisely how each spouse supports the other. Requiring a native Q/K hand actor plus foreign hand spouse spends both cards and can consume an entire two-card hand; it also collides with Q rank versus Queen role. This is too much new relationship teaching for an optional first-core route. Defer rather than invent a marriage on the basis of rank alone. If kept, distinguish historical role from game rank, name every breakage consequence, prevent sponsorship chains and test both spouse removals before/after succession.

No tactic above justifies restoring Help/Challenge/Cover in a core without History objects. Deferred full History must eventually be re-authored under the same card economy with finite timing proof; an unchanged seal implementation is not a compatible mode.

## Final cross-review closure — integrated bargain and marriage candidate

Following parent synthesis, the definitive recommendation is revised to retain the previously prioritized bargaining interaction and one tightly integrated marriage route. This supersedes the immediately preceding recommendation to defer all Trade/marriage. It does not adopt rank bands, Scout, Withdraw or readiness. The chosen core vocabulary is Recruit, Recall, Defend, Trade, native Name heir, and the foreign Marry and name heir alternative; all are printed/contextual card abilities. Pass and replenishment remain timing procedures.

**Trade:** any hand card may be the offered card. Show it, name one exact requested suit/rank and choose a different seat not formally approached this round. Requesting a card publicly known to be outside that seat's hand is invalid. Recipient may decline without proving possession; decline ends this opportunity as Pass and marks the recipient approached. Acceptance binds both exact identities immediately. Each exchanged card goes to its recipient's Spent area until the next boundary. Only the initiator may instead immediately Recruit the received card if native and strictly lower ranked than the offered card. This alternative enters Court, does not also initiate a Crown claim, and remains subject to ordinary office recovery. No recipient deployment bonus. The initiator cannot retract after seeing acceptance. The rank comparison buys public development at the cost of a higher concealed card and helps another person; it is not a guaranteed beneficial bargain.

**Marry and name heir:** foreign hand card is an optional alternative to the native Name heir card. Requires vacant Crown, your native ruler and an existing native unmarried Court Queen. The Queen becomes the named Witness and the foreign card the named heir. Their ranks must be equal or adjacent, with no A/K wraparound. The foreign card enters Court, creates a visible pair and immediately starts the same Crown sequence as native Name heir. Queen is the printed historical marriage role, never inferred from rank Q. Both cards must be otherwise unmarried; actor is the foreign hand card and is the entire initiation payment. The ruler, Queen and heir are three distinct people. There is no separate nonmatching marriage action in this core and no extra hidden setup step.

The native Name heir path remains available at every native rank with the ordinary native Witness prerequisite. The foreign path is useful because a native Court Queen plus a particular foreign rank gives an additional succession resource; its Queen relationship is also an additional exposed dependency. Both paths use the same single full successor-round clock. Do not grant the foreign route a different clock, points or added action allowance.

**Support and failure closure:** while the foreign heir is in Court it depends on the named Queen's presence. The pair persists after transfer. Removing the Queen breaks the pair, sends the now-unsupported foreign person to that controller's Spent area and fails the claim immediately. Removing the foreign heir breaks the pair and fails the claim; Queen remains supported in Court. No sponsorship chain or re-pairing restores a failed claim. Through transfer require original ruler, Witness and heir. After transfer require the new crowned ruler and Witness; the original native ruler is no longer a claim dependency. The foreign ruler remains subject to Queen support after transfer. If that ruler leaves, select a lawful native replacement by an explicit deterministic rule or subsequent native Recruit; never silently appoint another unsupported foreign person.

**Additional frozen tests:** accepted lower-native Trade enters Court while equal/higher remains Spent; rejecting Trade preserves both hands and reveals no recipient identity; request marks recipient regardless of acceptance; native receipt cannot claim in same exchange; exact foreign equal/neighbor marriage succeeds; gap two and A/K wraparound fail; a non-Queen Q fails; a Queen of any actual printed rank qualifies if comparison fits; already married Queen fails; foreign/non-native Queen fails; all three claim IDs distinct; each spouse departure before/after transfer resolves support and Crown in one atomic result; unsupported foreign person goes to Spent once; native fallback remains possible when no matching foreign spouse exists; former ruler removal after transfer does not fail otherwise valid native or marriage claim.

**Finite measure under closure:** Recruit, Recall, native/foreign heir initiation and defense remove one available hand card; Trade removes the offered and requested cards from both hands, including when the received native enters Court directly. No selected action returns a card to playable hand before boundary. Sum of available hands strictly decreases on every committed initiation, so at most the round's initial total hand count initiating actions occur (responses consume that same supply). Declined trades count as Pass and cannot indefinitely continue after finite committed actions end. Spent returns and two-card refill happen exactly once per completed boundary. The public twelve-round cap bounds total prototype length, with frequent cap draws remaining a failed strategic/pacing gate.

This closure supplies rank-sensitive bargaining and rank-sensitive foreign succession without unique-rank locks on native victory. It is a coherent candidate for implementation, not proof those routes are equally attractive. Observe actual offers/refusals and native versus marriage claim decisions in the ten-game record before declaring the additional complexity worthwhile.
